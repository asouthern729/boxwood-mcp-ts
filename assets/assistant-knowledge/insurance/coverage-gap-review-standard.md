# Boxwood Coverage Gap Review: the 11-step standard

Boxwood's standard review for a commercial account's coverage gaps. It checks consistency across policies (named insureds, terms, locations), policy content (limits vs. exposures, exclusions, limitations) and coordination (umbrella underlying, missing lines), and ends with questions to validate with the client.

Rate every finding with exactly one status:

- **GAP:** uninsured exposure confirmed from the documents
- **CONFIRM:** a possible gap that needs a client or carrier answer
- **OK:** checked, no issue
- **N/A:** doesn't apply

Give each GAP/CONFIRM a priority: **High** (could leave a core operation or a severe loss uninsured), **Medium** (meaningful dollars or a coverage condition the client must manage), **Low** (housekeeping or cost item). Every finding records: coverage part, finding, operational impact, action, citation (document name + form number).

### Step 1: Document inventory and account profile
List the policies reviewed (carrier, number, lines). Summarize operations and key exposures from section 1.3.

### Step 2: Named insured consistency
- Table every first named insured, additional named insured and DBA by policy, and compile one list of every entity name that appears.
- Flag: a policy that omits the primary operating entity; an entity shown as an LLC on one policy and a DBA on another; spelling variants; entities known to exist that appear on no policy.
- Impact: if the entities are legally separate, a policy naming only one may not respond for the other.

### Step 3: Policy period alignment
- Extract start, end and time reference for every policy. Group them by term and count the days between groups.
- Flag: umbrella/excess on a different term from its underlying; related coverages (e.g., garage liability vs garagekeepers) renewing on different dates; short-term policies; mixed time references.
- Action: confirm the offset is intentional, and recommend aligning terms at the next renewal.

### Step 4: Location schedule reconciliation
- Build one matrix: Address | Property | GL/Garage | WC | Other policies | Notes. Mark mailing-only addresses as "Mailing".
- Pull addresses from every other policy too (auto garaging, inland marine/dealer lot, bailee, umbrella, package).
- Flag: a location on Property but missing from GL or WC; the main office shown only as a mailing address on Property; address variants (Blvd vs Pkwy, ZIP differences); states of operation missing from WC 3.A/3.C; GL scheduled for one state when operations exist in others.
- Keep vehicle-garaging-only addresses separate from business premises.

### Step 5: Limits and sublimits vs exposures
Compare each limit to the exposure it protects. Report only limits that look too low, plus any same-coverage limit that differs sharply between two policies for the same insured.

| Coverage | Compare against | Typical red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Business Income / Extra Expense | Revenue, continuing expenses, restoration time | Less than ~12 months of income; differs by location or policy |
| Building / BPP / Inventory | Replacement cost, peak inventory | Coinsurance penalty risk; stale values |
| Per-item / per-unit limits | Highest single-item value | Any item above the per-unit maximum |
| GL / Garage Liability | Revenue, public exposure, contract requirements | $1M/$2M on a high-revenue or high-severity operation |
| Auto Liability | Fleet, passengers, rental | $1M CSL with passenger or rental exposure |
| Umbrella / Excess | Worst-case severity, contracts | $1-2M on a mid-size or high-severity account |
| Bailee / Garagekeepers | Value and count of customer property | More units on site than the per-loss maximum |
| Crime / Employee Dishonesty | Revenue, cash handling, headcount | $25-50K on a multimillion-dollar operation |
| Cyber | Records, payments, revenue | Low suite limits; endorsement only |
| EBL | Employee count | Aggregate low for headcount |
| Medical Payments | Public or passenger traffic | $5K default with heavy public exposure |
| Specialty sublimits (false pretense, spoilage, valuable papers, etc.) | Single plausible loss | Sublimit below one loss |

These are judgment flags, not rules. Phrase them as "may be low" with the comparison figure, and don't present them as definitive recommendations.

### Step 6: Named exclusions
Find exclusions that name a specific location, operation, project, person, entity or state (designated premises/operations/work, territorial, premises-specific roof or cosmetic wind/hail, safeguards tied to named locations). For a coverage part with none, record "No named exclusions found" so it's clear it was checked.

### Step 7: Exclusions that hit the insured's operations
List the revenue-producing activities (sales, service, repair, rental, installation, delivery, advice). For each one, check every relevant policy for exclusions that remove it. Examples: rental/lease/loan-to-others exclusions; flood or quake at temporary locations; professional or E&O exclusions on GL or umbrella; state or location exclusions; care-custody-control and contractual limits. State the impact in operational terms ("rentals to groups are not covered").

### Step 8: Limitations, conditions, deductibles
- **Property:** cause-of-loss form (Basic/Broad/Special); ACV vs RC; coinsurance; wind/hail and named-storm deductibles; vacancy; protective safeguards; pre-existing damage; sublimited extensions; cyber/cannabis exclusions.
- **Inland marine / dealer / bailee:** margin or reporting clauses; per-unit deductibles; maximum units per loss; notice periods.
- **Liability:** deductibles/SIRs (indemnity vs expense); non-stacking endorsements; underlying-insurance limitation endorsements on the umbrella; terrorism.
- **Premium side:** audit noncompliance charges; minimum earned premium; experience mod.

Read the forms schedule. Searching endorsement titles for "limitation" is not enough.

### Step 9: Umbrella / excess schedule of underlying
- If there's no umbrella and the account has auto, premises or employee exposure, record a CONFIRM.
- For each scheduled item, check that the policy is present and that the policy number, term and limits match (including minimum underlying requirements).
- Also check for what *should* be scheduled but isn't: GL, auto/garage liability, **employers liability (WC Part 2)**, EBL, liquor, garagekeepers if required.

### Step 10: Coordination and missing lines
- For every exclusion found in Steps 6-8, record whether it's picked up elsewhere ("covered under [policy]") or not.
- In multi-state or multi-entity programs, confirm that operations excluded on one policy are actually picked up on another.
- Check for lines the operation needs but doesn't carry: E&O, cyber, EPLI, D&O, pollution, hired/non-owned auto, contractor's equipment, etc.
- If contract requirements are known, check additional insured, waiver of subrogation and primary/non-contributory wording.

### Step 11: Validation questions
Turn every CONFIRM into one plain-language question the producer can ask the client, e.g., "Are X LLC and X Sales LLC separate legal entities?" or "Do any units exceed $250,000 in value?"
